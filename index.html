Application Guide

How to apply successfully

  1. 1

    Apply early: At least 2–3 months out

    Most Nairobi companies fill attachment slots two to three months before an intake starts. If a company has rolling openings, don't wait for "the right time", Apply Now.

  2. 2

    Prepare five documents in advance

    Introduction letter from your institution, an updated CV, a cover letter, a copy of your National ID, and your transcripts. Keep them as PDFs, ready to send instantly.

  3. 3

    Tailor every cover letter

    Name the company, reference a specific product or project of theirs, and explain how your skills fit. Generic letters get dismissed before anyone reads the second line.

  4. 4

    Use more than one channel

    Don't rely on a single job board. Email HR directly, check LinkedIn, revisit careers pages weekly, and call to confirm your application was received.

  5. 5

    Build a small portfolio

    A GitHub profile with two or three small projects, a website, a script or a database sets you apart. Link it in your CV. GitHub Pages and Netlify both host it for free.

  6. 6

    Target diploma-friendly institutions first

    Government agencies, county offices, and NGOs are consistently the most diploma-friendly. SACCOs and hospital ICT departments are reliable too.

What you'll need

  • Introduction letter — from your institution on official letterhead, signed by HOD or Dean
  • Updated CV — one to two pages, highlighting ICT skills, projects and academic performance
  • Cover letter — specific to the company and role
  • Academic transcripts — current results or marksheets
  • Copy of National ID — or Student ID
  • Passport photo — for government institutions
  • Insurance / consent form — some institutions, especially hospitals, require this
  • Full-time availability — for the entire attachment period, typically three months
  • Police clearance certificate — required by some government bodies and banks
  • Signed attachment agreement — from your institution's industrial attachment coordinator

Pro tip: Government institutions like KRA, Kenya Power and KenGen take applications strictly online; don't email or walk in. Smaller tech companies and startups are the opposite a walk-in with printed documents and a USB of your portfolio can work well.
